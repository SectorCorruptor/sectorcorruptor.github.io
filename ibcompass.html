MYP stages

The Leap: starting MYP 1.

🚀 The Leap · MYP 1 🌉 The Bridge · MYP 2→3 🧭 The Choice · MYP 3→4

Change hits weirdly. One day your schedule is simple, the next it's stacked and moving faster than you expect. You're suddenly dealing with more subjects, more switches, more things happening at once. It's not deep or emotional, it's just different. The pace shifts, the expectations shift, and you end up adjusting because there's no real alternative. You follow what's in front of you, you move to the next thing.

"You don't need to have it figured out on day one. You just need a planner and a decent night's sleep."

What Changes

  • You'll have a different teacher and room for most subjects.
  • Grades become criteria-based — you're scored on skills labelled A, B, C, D, not one overall mark.
  • You get a planner (paper or app) and start building ATL skills — that's "learning how to learn," like organizing your time.
  • Homework comes from several teachers at once, so deadlines can stack up.

What Stays the Same

  • You still learn through inquiry-based units built around a big question.
  • You're still studying all eight MYP subject groups.
  • Service as Action — helping your community — continues, just like before.
  • Group work, projects, and hands-on learning are still the norm.

Your First 30 Days

  • Week 1Write every teacher's name and room number in one place. Set up your planner.
  • Week 2Find one subject that feels tricky and ask a teacher one specific question about it.
  • Week 3Start a "due dates" habit — check your planner every evening, even for five seconds.
  • Week 4Look back at your first graded task. Notice one thing you'd do differently next time.

‹ HomeNext: The Bridge ›

MYP 3 is the year things stop being handed to you in small pieces. The tasks stretch out, the deadlines spread wider, and teachers expect you to manage your own time instead of waiting for reminders. At some point, the choices you'll make later start forming gradually. This is not really a warning or something too big, it's simply the point where school shifts from following instructions to figuring out how you work.

"Bigger tasks aren't a punishment. They're practice for choosing your own path."

What Changes

  • Assignments get longer and span more weeks — you'll need to break them into chunks yourself.
  • Teachers expect more independence: fewer reminders, more "you've got this."
  • You'll do a mini practice run of independent research, warming you up for the real Personal Project later.
  • Subject options for MYP 4–5 start getting mentioned — worth paying attention to.

What Stays the Same

  • Still eight subject groups, still criteria-based grading.
  • Still inquiry-based units with a central big idea.
  • ATL skills and Service as Action keep going, now at a higher gear.

Your First 30 Days

  • Week 1Look at your longest assignment and split it into three smaller deadlines.
  • Week 2Ask a teacher what "independent work" looks like in their class.
  • Week 3Start noticing which subjects you actually enjoy — you'll want that later.
  • Week 4Try planning one whole week in advance instead of day by day.

‹ The LeapNext: The Choice ›

Welcome to the big one. At the end of MYP 3, you'll choose a subject pathway for MYP 4–5: Balanced or Sciences. It's not something you have to stick with forever. Most options stay open no matter which one you pick, unless you drop a subject from MYP 4, as long as you understand what each pathway leads toward. This isn't about predicting your future at 14–15, it's about choosing the kind of thinking you want to work with for the next two years.

"Pick the pathway that matches how your brain likes to operate, not the one that sounds impressive when you say it out loud."

What Changes

  • You pick a pathway: Balanced or Sciences, shaping which subject options you take.
  • Fewer subjects overall, but each one goes deeper.
  • Your choices start connecting to what comes after the MYP, like the Diploma Programme.

What Stays the Same

  • You still take Language & Literature, Language Acquisition, and Maths no matter what.
  • Criteria-based grading and ATL skills continue as normal.
  • You can still switch focus later — this isn't permanent.

Your First 30 Days

  • Week 1List three subjects you look forward to and three you'd rather skip.
  • Week 2Try the "Which Doors Stay Open?" checker below with a career idea you're curious about.
  • Week 3Talk to a teacher or counselor about the pathway you're leaning toward.
  • Week 4Take the full Subject Quiz on its own page (it's in the menu at the top).

      IB transitions, year by year

      From PYP all the way to the Diploma Programme: what changes at each step.

      1. PYP 5 → MYP 1

        • A big jump in structure.
        • Subjects become separate instead of blended together.
        • Grading becomes criteria-based.
        • More homework and more projects.
        • ATL skills like time management and research start to matter.

      2. MYP 2 → MYP 3

        • The workload goes up.
        • More independent tasks.
        • Science and maths get harder.
        • Teachers expect clearer explanations and reasoning.

      3. MYP 3 → MYP 4

        • The first "high school" level jump.
        • You start choosing subjects.
        • Assessments get more serious and criteria expectations get stricter.
        • Planning for the Personal Project begins.

      4. MYP 4 → MYP 5

        • The heaviest workload in the MYP.
        • The Personal Project is a major focus.
        • MYP eAssessments, if your school takes part.
        • DP subject choices start here.

      5. MYP 5 → DP 1

        • Six subjects, some at Higher Level and some at Standard Level.
        • The DP core starts: TOK, the Extended Essay and CAS.
        • Final exams and grades now count toward university.

      The MYP in a nutshell

      11–16age group
      5 yearsMYP 1 to MYP 5
      8subject groups
      50+ hoursper subject, per year

      The 8 subject groups

      • Language & Literature
      • Language Acquisition
      • Individuals & Societies
      • Sciences
      • Mathematics
      • Arts
      • Design
      • Physical & Health Education

      How learning works

      Concept-based: you learn big ideas, not just facts to memorise.

      Global contexts: every unit links to one of six real-world themes: identities and relationships; orientation in space and time; personal and cultural expression; scientific and technical innovation; globalization and sustainability; fairness and development.

      ATL skills: five skill areas — thinking, research, communication, social and self-management.

      How you're assessed

      Most assessment happens in school, by your own teachers.

      Some schools also take the optional MYP eAssessment in MYP 5: onscreen exams plus ePortfolios of coursework. It leads to the IB's MYP Certificate.

      The IB's own research reports that MYP students tend to manage their learning well, connect lessons to the real world, and do well later in the DP.

      Choosing subjects for MYP 4–5

      The IB lets schools cut down from 8 subject groups to 6 in MYP 4–5. At our school you do that by picking the Balanced or Sciences pathway.

      How to choose smartly

      • Pick subjects that lead toward the DP courses you might want.
      • Choose what you can keep up with for two whole years.
      • Mix it up: roughly two heavy, two medium and two lighter subjects.
      • Avoid stacking only high-content subjects. That's how burnout happens.

      Example focus subjects by goal

      STEM

      Maths, Physics, Chemistry, Design

      Medicine

      Biology, Chemistry, Maths, Language & Literature

      Business

      Business Studies or Economics, Maths, Language & Literature, Design

      Humanities

      Individuals & Societies, Language & Literature, Arts, Language Acquisition

      Formative vs summative

      Formative = practice

      • Helps you learn and get feedback.
      • Usually doesn't count toward your final grade.
      • Examples: classwork, small quizzes, drafts.
      vs

      Summative = performance

      • The graded task at the end of a unit.
      • Counts toward your term grade, marked on the criteria.
      • Examples: tests, essays, lab reports, projects.

      Teachers use both to see how you're growing. Treat formatives as free practice for the summative.

      How MYP grading works

      You're not graded by percentage. You're graded on the quality of your skills, one criterion at a time.

      Step by step

      1. Each subject has four criteria: A, B, C and D.
      2. You get a level from 0 to 8 on each one.
      3. The four levels are added up, out of 32.
      4. That total converts into a final grade from 1 to 7.

      What the levels mean

      1–2 Limited3–4 Adequate5–6 Substantial7–8 Excellent

      The big projects

      Personal Project MYP 5

      1. Choose a topic you care about and set a learning goal.
      2. Research and plan.
      3. Create a product.
      4. Write a report about the process.

      Takes at least 25 hours. Assessed on three criteria, each out of 8: A Planning, B Applying skills, C Reflecting.

      Interdisciplinary Unit (IDU)

      Combines two or more subjects in one unit, so you can see how ideas connect in the real world.

      Community Project MYP 3 or 4

      A project that meets a real need in a community. Some schools run it before the Personal Project.

      Design projects

      Every design task follows the design cycle:

      After the MYP: the Diploma Programme

      16–19age group
      6subjects
      3core parts
      45top possible score

      Your six subjects

      One from each of these groups:

      • Language & Literature
      • Language Acquisition
      • Individuals & Societies
      • Sciences
      • Mathematics

      The sixth is an Arts subject, or a second subject from one of the groups above.

      The core

      TOK (Theory of Knowledge): thinking about how we know what we know.

      EE (Extended Essay): a 4,000-word research essay on a topic you choose.

      CAS (Creativity, Activity, Service): projects and activities outside the classroom.

      Scoring

      Each subject is graded 1 to 7, so six subjects give up to 42 points.

      TOK and the EE together add up to 3 more, for a maximum of 45.

      The Personal Project is the best practice you'll get for the Extended Essay.
